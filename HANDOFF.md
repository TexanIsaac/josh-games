session on 29 to 30 July 2026, shipped at v55. This is the state of it, why it is built
the way it is, and what a fresh thread needs to know before touching it.

## What it is

Josh's design, unchanged throughout and not to be altered without him:

- You start as a noob with fists, fighting zombies.
- Zombies bite you; enough bites and you become one. Then you bite noobs.
- Four ranks a side, earned at **7 / 22 / 37 kills**. Mirrored on both sides.
- There is no game over. You go back and forth between the sides forever.

Everything else is scaffolding around that and can be changed freely.

## How it is built

- One HTML file, no dependencies, no build step. Runs from `file://` or from Pages.
- Canvas 2D with a hand written perspective camera: yaw, pitch, distance, focal length,
  perspective divide. `isoX`/`isoY` project world to screen; `viewDepth` sorts.
- Everything solid is a turnable box (`drawBoxRot`), depth sorted, flat shaded per face.
- BFS flow field pathfinding, rebuilt towards the player. `sealPockets` fills anything
  unreachable so a wave can never be held open by something stranded.
- Sound is ZzFX (MIT, Frank Force, credited in source and CREDITS).
- Progress is in `localStorage`: coins, look, perks, medals, per-difficulty bests.

### Map and terrain

72 by 44 tiles, generated by a script rather than typed. Tile kinds: wall, crate,
barrel, sandbag/rubble, hedgehog/spikes, door, mountain, trench, two storey block.

- **Mountains** are indestructible; a nuke leaves them standing. Tested with eight nukes.
- **Trenches** are holes: negative height, so you drop in and climb out. They count as
  walkable for the pathfinder or a trench system would be walled off.
- **Doors** swing open when anything is near and are passable to the pathfinder even
  when shut, otherwise a room behind one gets sealed and its occupants stranded.
- **Buildings** are rooms with doors on opposite walls and an inner dividing wall.

### Characters: three attempts, and why the third works

This ate most of the session and is the thing most likely to be revisited.

1. **Twenty small boxes**, each rounded, shaded and outlined on its own. At ninety
   pixels tall each box is about ten pixels. Twenty independently shaded ten pixel
   blobs read as gravel. Josh said "terrible" every time, and was right every time.
2. **Flat billboard**, one figure facing the camera, mirrored for direction. Readable,
   but not 3D, and Josh spotted that immediately.
3. **Eight big blocks in real 3D**, which is what Roblox does: head, torso, two arms,
   two legs, headgear. Each a real box, turned to face the walk direction, flat shaded
   per face with a strong step between faces. The face is painted on the flat front
   plane of the head so it stays stuck on from any angle.

**The lesson: the problem was never that they were boxes. It was that there were twenty
tiny ones.** If this is revisited, that is the thing to hold on to.

A bug worth remembering: after building the blocky rig, `drawGuy` was still switching on
the rounded painter, so the blocky look never reached the screen at all. Found while
measuring performance, not by looking. Nothing in the tests could have caught it.

## The recurring bug class

**Six bugs this session had one shape: a number that was only correct because of another
number, with the relationship written down nowhere.**

| Bug | The hidden relationship |
|---|---|
| See over the top of doors | door height vs wall height |
| See past doors at the sides | leaf thickness vs wall bar thickness |
| Walk out of the map over a roof | boundary height vs tallest climbable thing |
| Enemies shot on arrival, no crowds | spawn ring vs longest weapon reach |
| Void visible at the edges when zoomed out | ground skirt vs maximum zoom |
| First person camera at chin height | eye height vs character height |

All six are now derived from each other in code rather than independently guessed. **If a
new number is added that has to agree with an existing one, derive it.** That single
habit would have prevented every one of these.

The last two were found by going looking for the pattern rather than waiting to be told,
and neither had broken yet: both were one small change away.

## Testing

`node tests/run.js`, 682 checks, plain assertions with a fake DOM and a canvas stub.
It has caught real bugs repeatedly, including several that were invisible by eye.

Things worth knowing:

- The canvas context is a `let` with a `setCtx` hook so tests can hand in a counting
  stand-in and **measure** frame cost. A frame with 34 enemies is 6,910 canvas
  operations, budget 7,400.
- That budget test **was flaky** and the flakiness was the interesting part: it spawned
  enemies randomly, so the scene differed each run and it failed about one run in five.
  A test that fails one run in five teaches you to ignore it. It measures a fixed scene
  now and gives an identical number every time.
- Prefer tests that **trigger the behaviour** over tests that check a comment exists. The
  medal tests kill an actual enemy; the popper test puts a neighbour beside one and
  checks it took damage while one further away did not.
- A test guarding another test's validity caught a real gameplay bug: when the spawn ring
  fell inside weapon range, crowds stopped forming and the clumping measurement dropped
  from 105 samples to one. Nothing else would have noticed.

## Content as it stands

- **11 weapons**, 120 to 2,600 coins, priced against measured income (~400 coins by wave
  5, ~1,075 by wave 10, ~3,250 by wave 20). Damage deliberately does not track price;
  dearer means more specialised.
- **16 upgrades**, ~12,700 coins for all levels. Everything together is ~22,000, a
  deliberate long tail.
- **16 medals**, shown locked with their hint so there is always a visible next thing.
- **5 enemy kinds**: walker, runner, brute, popper (explodes on death, hits its own side
  too), leaper (rushes the last stretch).
- **6 special waves**, every third wave: swarm, heavy, all leapers, all poppers,
  nightfall, payday. Never the same one twice running.
- **4 difficulties**, multipliers on the tuned numbers so the levels stay in proportion.
- **6 anthems**, all real recordings, all verified public domain. Provenance in
  `CREDITS.md`. The Nazi party song is deliberately excluded and the reason is recorded
  in the source; do not add it.

## Open items

**Needs Josh's eyes, not more building.** Several things are verified correct and fast
but nobody has judged whether they look or feel right:

- The blocky characters. Levers if wrong: chunkier proportions, or a harder step between
  face shades. Both single numbers.
- Whether the tripled map feels big or empty.
- Whether wave 3 being a special wave is too soon. One character to change.
- Whether the flowers and rainbows land as funny (about 1 shot in 25).

**Not started:** camo clothing option; in-game weapon models matching the shop cards
exactly; more boss variety.

**Known and accepted:** inner walls are drawn about a third of a tile thick but still
block the whole tile, so there is a visual gap between what you see and what stops you.
Josh asked for thin walls twice; this is the trade.

**Unresolved:** a crash on v29 that showed as "Script error, line 0, column 0" was never
root caused. Safari withheld the message. The crash handler now reports the real error
and stack, and the frame loop survives a throw, so if it recurs it will name itself.
Josh has said he does not care about it.

## Gotchas for a fresh session

- **Patch with Python scripts using exact anchors, and assert the anchor matches exactly
  once.** Sed and heredocs repeatedly mangled quoting. Escaped newlines inside JS string
  literals in the test file broke it four separate times.
- **Always** extract the `<script>` and run `node --check` after editing, before testing.
- Bump both `VERSION` in `index.html` and `CACHE` in `sw.js`, or the service worker
  serves a stale copy.
- Verify a deploy by reading `VERSION` back off the live URL. Pages lags the push.
- `const` declarations must come before anything that reads them; one constant was
  declared below its first use and the whole game failed to start.
- Do not audit or change BoM Builder or Pulse from this project; unrelated.
